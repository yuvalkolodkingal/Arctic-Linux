Meet Arctic Linux.
A calm, keyboard-first desktop built on Fedora, with the Mango tiling window manager.
Let's take a look at installation, the lighter everyday apps, and a few details that make the desktop your own.
Start with a live USB.
The boot menu lets you try Arctic Linux or open the installer.
You can explore the desktop before deciding to put it on your computer.
The live welcome card gives you a few useful shortcuts.
Keep trying the desktop, or choose Install Arctic Linux.
The install button is also right there on the top bar.
The installer starts with your language.
Each screen asks one clear question, and the sidebar shows where you are.
These installer screens use sample disks, networks, and account details.
Choose your keyboard layout, then try it in the test box.
The live session uses your choice too.
If you switch to a text console or disconnect a screen, the installer keeps your page and your answers.
Connect to Wi-Fi, or plug in a cable.
You can also choose Install offline.
The apps already on the image remain available, while extra downloads wait until you're connected.
Your new system remembers the Wi-Fi connection.
Next, check your time zone.
The installer suggests a location, and you can change the region or city.
Automatic time keeps the clock in sync.
Choose your disk and how to install.
Erasing replaces its contents.
Installing alongside another system is offered when suitable free space is available.
Back up your files before you continue.
Disk encryption is on by default.
Create a passphrase, or use the suggested one.
You'll enter it when the computer starts.
Keep it safe: forgotten passphrases cannot be recovered.
Create your account, set your sign-in password, and name the computer.
Automatic login is optional.
You can also choose to use your account password for the encrypted disk.
The suggested apps now keep the everyday setup lighter: GNOME Web for browsing, Foot for the terminal, PCManFM for files, FeatherPad for text, and Celluloid for media.
Choose alternatives if you prefer.
Office suites and code editors are optional.
The summary brings your choices together.
Check the disk, encryption, account, and apps, and change anything you need.
Nothing is written to the disk until you confirm the install here.
The progress screen shows the system and your selected apps being installed.
An optional app download can be retried or skipped.
VoxType setup runs separately, so a missing model never stops the operating system from installing.
When installation finishes, read the final notes.
They tell you about queued downloads and any Secure Boot steps for a driver.
Remove the USB and restart when you're ready.
These screens are a demonstration, rather than a recording of a fresh install.
On the desktop, five workspaces give each task some room.
The bar stays small, and the backgrounds now come from the project's photo collection.
Existing users keep their own wallpaper choices when they update.
Press Super and Space to open the launcher.
Type an app name and press Enter.
It also finds files, open windows, and settings, so you can get to the next task quickly.
The launcher handles small jobs too.
Start with an equals sign to calculate, and press Enter to copy the answer.
You can convert units, run commands, or set a reminder for later.
Open a few windows and Mango arranges them automatically.
The amber border marks the one you're using.
Move between windows with Super and the arrow keys, or switch workspaces to separate your tasks.
PCManFM is the new graphical file manager, using Fedora's GTK three build.
Browse folders and downloads in tabs, and use XArchiver to open or create archives.
Super and F opens whichever file manager you've chosen.
Super and Enter opens Foot with Fish and the Arctic fox greeting.
Bash and Nano are there too.
Fedora packages, Flatpak and Nix give you several ways to add tools, without making a large app collection compulsory.
GNOME Web shares the WebKit engine already used by Arctic's web apps.
FeatherPad handles everyday text, and Celluloid plays music and video.
Chromium remains available for web apps that need calling support.
You can change the defaults in Settings.
Need something else?
Open Get apps and choose Flathub, Fedora packages, a web app, or a terminal tool.
Installations continue when you close the launcher.
Removing software has its own menu, so it's easier to find the action you want.
Open Remove apps from the launcher to manage Fedora packages, Flatpaks, web apps, and terminal entries.
Arctic shows the proposed removal before you confirm.
It protects the desktop, your login shell, and your only terminal from an accidental removal.
Arctic installs pinned, signature-checked official VoxType 1.1.0 binaries and a verified multilingual Whisper model.
During an online installation, Arctic automatically downloads and verifies the compatible CPU engine and model.
An offline installation leaves a clear retry for first boot and reports that dictation is not ready.
The local multilingual model supports Hebrew and English without sending audio to a service.
Recording remains unavailable until the engine, model, and per-user VoxType configuration are ready.
Focus a text field and press Arctic's Mango shortcut, whose readiness guard runs VoxType's official record toggle.
Press the same shortcut again to stop, transcribe, and insert with VoxType.
Arctic's dictation pill follows VoxType's recording and transcription state, even when the taskbar is hidden.
Audio and transcription stay on this computer.
VoxType runs in quiet mode, and Arctic's notifications and status output do not include transcript text.
VoxType uses one local multilingual Whisper model for Hebrew, English, and automatic language detection.
Supported hardware can enable VoxType's Vulkan backend.
If GPU setup fails, the error stays visible and the user can explicitly switch VoxType to CPU.
In a hosted Westmere CPU test, the small model measured 7.77 percent English word error and 42.0 percent Hebrew word error.
It took 15.79 times the English audio duration and 11.63 times the Hebrew duration, so that CPU result was not real time.
Super and S opens Arctic Settings.
Search for a setting instead of hunting through pages.
Changes apply straight away, and Control and Z undoes the last one.
Put the bar on any edge, and choose always visible, auto-hide, or dodge windows.
Move the pointer to its edge, or use the keyboard, to reveal it.
Arctic's dictation indicator remains visible.
The installer keeps its bar available while you're in the live session.
Choose Winter or Polar night, or let a wallpaper provide the desktop's colours.
The palette follows through the shell, window borders, terminal, and supported apps.
Your own pictures stay selected when you change themes.
Winter keeps the same layout with soft, snow-coloured surfaces.
The wallpaper picker includes the project's growing photo collection, alongside pictures you add yourself.
You can set a favourite, or let the wallpaper change on a schedule.
Built-in wallpapers can be deleted from your view and restored later, without affecting another account.
One new choice is Ember Clouds, City Silhouette: warm clouds above the dark outline of the city.
It's the artist's finished photograph, preserved at its original resolution.
Set it from the wallpaper picker, or browse the full collection on the project's gallery.
Quick settings puts the everyday controls together: volume, brightness, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and power mode.
Night light makes evenings more comfortable, while Keep awake is handy during a presentation or long task.
The network menu lets you join Wi-Fi and manage saved connections or a VPN.
Nearby bar menus handle Bluetooth devices, speakers, microphones, and sound levels for individual apps.
At your desk, arrange monitors and adjust their resolution, scale, and rotation.
Super and P switches screen modes.
Arctic also handles USB drives, printing, and scanning, with settings when you need them.
You don't have to memorise every shortcut.
Super and slash opens a searchable guide.
Settings lets you add your own shortcuts, choose default apps, and decide what starts when you sign in.
Arctic's signed stable repository keeps existing systems up to date.
Updates download in the background and install when you restart.
After each update, What's New shows the changes once, with release notes ready to reopen later.
Settings shows the latest check and anything waiting.
Save your work before choosing Restart and install.
There is no need to reinstall for desktop package updates.
Super and L locks the desktop.
The clock stays visible, and notifications show a count without their contents.
Arctic also locks before sleeping.
Your session stays protected when you step away.
That's Arctic Linux: a guided installer, a calm desktop, useful native apps, and local dictation when setup is complete.
