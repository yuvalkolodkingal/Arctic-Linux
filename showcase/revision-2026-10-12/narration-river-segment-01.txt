Meet Arctic Linux. A calm, keyboard-first desktop built on Fedora, with the Mango tiling window manager. Let's take a look at installation, the lighter everyday apps, and a few details that make the desktop your own.

Start with a live USB. The boot menu lets you try Arctic Linux or open the installer. You can explore the desktop before deciding to put it on your computer.

The live welcome card gives you a few useful shortcuts. Keep trying the desktop, or choose Install Arctic Linux. The install button is also right there on the top bar.

The installer starts with your language. Each screen asks one clear question, and the sidebar shows where you are. These installer screens use sample disks, networks, and account details.

Choose your keyboard layout, then try it in the test box. The live session uses your choice too. If you switch to a text console or disconnect a screen, the installer keeps your page and your answers.

Connect to Wi-Fi, or plug in a cable. You can also choose Install offline. The apps already on the image remain available, while extra downloads wait until you're connected. Your new system remembers the Wi-Fi connection.

Next, check your time zone. The installer suggests a location, and you can change the region or city. Automatic time keeps the clock in sync.

Choose your disk and how to install. Erasing replaces its contents. Installing alongside another system is offered when suitable free space is available. Back up your files before you continue.

Disk encryption is on by default. Create a passphrase, or use the suggested one. You'll enter it when the computer starts. Keep it safe: forgotten passphrases cannot be recovered.

Create your account, set your sign-in password, and name the computer. Automatic login is optional. You can also choose to use your account password for the encrypted disk.

The suggested apps now keep the everyday setup lighter: GNOME Web for browsing, Foot for the terminal, PCManFM for files, FeatherPad for text, and Celluloid for media. Choose alternatives if you prefer. Office suites and code editors are optional.

The summary brings your choices together. Check the disk, encryption, account, and apps, and change anything you need. Nothing is written to the disk until you confirm the install here.

The progress screen shows the system and your selected apps being installed. An optional app download can be retried or skipped. VoxType setup runs separately, so a missing model never stops the operating system from installing.

When installation finishes, read the final notes. They tell you about queued downloads and any Secure Boot steps for a driver. Remove the USB and restart when you're ready. These screens are a demonstration, rather than a recording of a fresh install.

On the desktop, five workspaces give each task some room. The bar stays small, and the backgrounds now come from the project's photo collection. Existing users keep their own wallpaper choices when they update.

Press Super and Space to open the launcher. Type an app name and press Enter. It also finds files, open windows, and settings, so you can get to the next task quickly.

The launcher handles small jobs too. Start with an equals sign to calculate, and press Enter to copy the answer. You can convert units, run commands, or set a reminder for later.

Open a few windows and Mango arranges them automatically. The amber border marks the one you're using. Move between windows with Super and the arrow keys, or switch workspaces to separate your tasks.

PCManFM is the new graphical file manager, using Fedora's GTK three build. Browse folders and downloads in tabs, and use XArchiver to open or create archives. Super and F opens whichever file manager you've chosen.

Super and Enter opens Foot with Fish and the Arctic fox greeting. Bash and Nano are there too. Fedora packages, Flatpak and Nix give you several ways to add tools, without making a large app collection compulsory.

GNOME Web shares the WebKit engine already used by Arctic's web apps. FeatherPad handles everyday text, and Celluloid plays music and video. Chromium remains available for web apps that need calling support. You can change the defaults in Settings.

Need something else? Open Get apps and choose Flathub, Fedora packages, a web app, or a terminal tool. Installations continue when you close the launcher. Removing software has its own menu, so it's easier to find the action you want.
