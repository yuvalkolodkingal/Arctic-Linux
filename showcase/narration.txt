Meet Arctic Linux. A calm, keyboard-first desktop built on Fedora, with the Mango tiling window manager. Let's go from the first boot to a day of getting things done.

Start with a live USB. The boot menu lets you try Arctic Linux or open the installer. You can explore the desktop before deciding to put it on your computer.

The live welcome card gives you a few useful shortcuts. Keep trying the desktop, or choose Install Arctic Linux. The install button is also right there on the top bar.

The installer starts with your language. Each screen asks one clear question, and the sidebar shows where you are. These installer screens use sample disks, networks, and account details.

Choose your keyboard layout, then try typing in the test box. Your choice also applies to the live session, so the passwords you enter use the layout you expect.

Connect to Wi-Fi, or use a network cable. Arctic downloads the apps you choose during installation, and carries your Wi-Fi settings into the installed system.

Next, check your time zone. The installer suggests a location, and you can change the region or city. Automatic time keeps the clock in sync.

Choose your disk and how to install. Erasing replaces its contents. Installing alongside another system is offered when suitable free space is available. Back up your files before you continue.

Disk encryption is on by default. Create a passphrase, or use the suggested one. You'll enter it when the computer starts. Keep it safe: forgotten passphrases cannot be recovered.

Create your account, set your sign-in password, and name the computer. Automatic login is optional. You can also choose to use your account password for the encrypted disk.

The app picker comes with suggested favourites, but the choices are yours. Pick your browser, editor, terminal, file manager, office tools, and more. Relevant hardware drivers appear when they're needed.

The summary brings your choices together. Check the disk, encryption, account, and apps, and change anything you need. Nothing is written to the disk until you confirm the install here.

The progress screen shows what's happening as Arctic sets up the system and installs your apps. If an optional app download fails, you can retry it or skip it and add it later.

When setup finishes, review the final notes, restart, and remove the USB. If a driver needs a Secure Boot key, the installer gives you the instructions for that first restart.

Now for everyday life on Arctic. The desktop keeps the bar small and the workspace open. Five workspaces help you separate tasks, and the apps you picked are ready to use.

Press Super and Space to open the launcher. Type an app name and press Enter. It also finds files, open windows, and settings, so you can get to the next task quickly.

The launcher handles small jobs too. Start with an equals sign to calculate, and press Enter to copy the answer. You can convert units, run commands, or set a reminder for later.

Open a few windows and Mango arranges them automatically. The amber border marks the one you're using. Move between windows with Super and the arrow keys, or switch workspaces to separate your tasks.

Nautilus, also known as GNOME Files, is the graphical file manager in this walkthrough. Browse documents and downloads beside your other windows. Open your chosen file manager with Super and F.

Super and Enter opens your terminal. The default setup pairs Kitty with Fish and the Arctic fox greeting. Flatpak and Nix are preinstalled, alongside Fedora's package tools.

For everyday apps, Arctic suggests Zen for browsing, Zed for editing, Collabora Office for documents, and VLC for media. These are choices, not requirements. You can replace them during installation or later in Settings.

Need something else? Open Get apps. Choose Flathub, Fedora packages, a web app, or a terminal tool. Installations keep going when you close the launcher, and you get a notification when an app is ready.

Super and S opens Arctic Settings. Search for a setting instead of hunting through pages. Changes apply straight away, and Control and Z undoes the last one.

Choose the light Winter theme or dark Polar night. Your own wallpaper can also provide the desktop's colours, carrying them through the shell, window borders, terminal, and supported apps.

Winter keeps the same layout, with soft snow-coloured surfaces. Open the wallpaper picker to choose Arctic artwork or your own pictures. Built-in wallpapers can follow the theme automatically.

Quick settings puts the everyday controls together: volume, brightness, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and power mode. Night light makes evenings more comfortable, while Keep awake is handy during a presentation or long task.

The network menu lets you join Wi-Fi and manage saved connections or a VPN. Nearby bar menus handle Bluetooth devices, speakers, microphones, and sound levels for individual apps.

At your desk, arrange monitors and adjust their resolution, scale, and rotation. Super and P switches screen modes. Arctic also handles USB drives, printing, and scanning, with settings when you need them.

You don't have to memorise every shortcut. Super and slash opens a searchable guide. Settings lets you add your own shortcuts, choose default apps, and decide what starts when you sign in.

Updates download in the background and install when the computer restarts. Settings shows what's waiting and lets you choose the update channel. System snapshots can help restore files after an update, and firmware updates are available when you ask.

When you step away, Super and L locks the screen. The power menu offers suspend, restart, and shut down. Arctic also locks before sleeping, with the timing controlled in Settings.

That's Arctic Linux: a guided installer, a calm desktop, and useful details for everyday work. Find the project, downloads, and full documentation on GitHub. Try it from a USB, and make it yours.
